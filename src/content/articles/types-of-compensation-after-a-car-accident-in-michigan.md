<p>There are generally two types of <a href="/ann-arbor-personal-injury-law-office/">personal injury</a> damages recoverable after a car accident. First, if an injured person satisfies the Michigan threshold requirement of serious impairment of body function or permanent serious disfigurement, the <a href="/">injured party</a> can be compensated for non-economic damages or injuries to the person. This consists of the following:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Physical pain and suffering</li>
  <li>Mental anguish</li>
  <li>Fright and shock</li>
  <li>Denial of social pleasure and enjoyment of life</li>
  <li>Embarrassment, humiliation or mortification</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other form of compensation is for economic damages, which does not require a finding of a threshold injury involving a Michigan resident and includes:</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Medical expenses for care and rehabilitation</li>
  <li>Attendant and nursing care for the injured person</li>
  <li>Past and future wage loss</li>
  <li>Property damage
    <ul>
      <li>Mini tort – up to $3,000.00 for repair or replacement of your car</li>
      <li>Collision repair damages depending on the type of coverage</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>
