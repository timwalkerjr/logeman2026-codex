<p>Michigan premises liability laws require that property owners maintain their properties and retail stores in a reasonably safe condition. When a property is not properly maintained in a safe condition and you are injured, you may be entitled to <a href="/articles/how-do-you-calculate-pain-suffering-damages-in-a-personal-injury-lawsuit">compensation for your injuries</a>.</p>

<h2>How to Prove Slip &amp; Fall Cases in Michigan</h2>
<ul>
  <li>You must prove that there is a dangerous condition that was the cause of your slip and fall injuries or damages. This may involve video or photographic evidence, as well as evidence of your injuries as documented in medical records.</li>
  <li>You must identify whether the property owner was responsible for the dangerous condition.</li>
  <li>You must prove that the responsible party either created — or was at the very least aware of — the dangerous condition that caused your slip and fall accident and failed to take steps to eliminate the hazardous condition.</li>
</ul>
<p>Proving a property owner to be completely at fault for a slip and fall can be difficult. Hiring an experienced slip and fall attorney to bring your case makes it more likely that you will win your case.</p>

<h2>Common Types of Slip and Fall Cases in Michigan</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Falls on ice or snow at retail businesses, apartment complexes, and residential sidewalks.</li>
  <li>Uneven sidewalks, steps without handrails, potholes and slippery conditions in store aisleways.</li>
  <li>Lack of handrails on stairway steps.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Average Slip and Fall Verdicts &amp; Settlements in Michigan</h2>
<p>Slip and fall settlements in Michigan and other areas can range depending on the severity of injuries and harm. The total amount received through compensation depends on variables including the strength of your lawsuit, and how much monetary value is calculated for the damages you suffered.</p>

<h2>Settlements Can Vary Due to Several Factors</h2>
<ul>
  <li>The county in which the slip and fall occurred.</li>
  <li>The costs of any medical bills.</li>
  <li>Lost income as a result of missed work.</li>
  <li>The severity of your pain and suffering from your incident.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Michigan Slip &amp; Fall Statute of Limitations</h2>
<p>Michigan statute of limitations for slip and fall cases is three years. This time limit starts on the date of your incident. It is critical that you take steps to find an experienced slip and fall or <a href="/about-us/">personal injury attorney</a> as soon as you can after your accident.</p>

<h2>Comparative Negligence</h2>
<p>Michigan has a comparative negligence rule that determines how much compensation a victim can recover from the property owner of the premises where they fell, based on the negligence of the victim at the time of their incident. Most often, property owners will defend any slip and fall lawsuit filed against them by claiming that the victim was at least partly at fault for any injuries sustained. This reduces the amount they will owe victims, or in rare cases, excuses them from all charges completely.</p>
<p><strong>However, if you are more than 50% at fault, you are not entitled to compensation for your non-economic damages.</strong></p>

<h2>Steps to Take After a Slip and Fall Accident</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Seek Medical Attention</strong>: It is important to seek medical attention. This ensures your health is protected and creates a medical record linking your injuries to the accident, which can be crucial in your legal case.</li>
  <li><strong>Take Photographs of the Accident Scene</strong>: This is important because the dangerous condition will be repaired or cleaned up shortly after your fall. Photos are the best way to prove that a hazard existed and caused your fall.</li>
  <li><strong>Photograph Your Injuries</strong>: Document that injuries were caused by the accident event.</li>
  <li><strong>Fill Out an Incident Report</strong>: Notify the property owner or manager immediately. Incident reports provide written documentation of the incident and are sent to the liability insurance company for the property owner.</li>
  <li><strong>Witness Information</strong>: Obtain the names and phone numbers of any eyewitnesses.</li>
</ul>
