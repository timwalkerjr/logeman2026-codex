<p>There are special rules that govern personal injury settlements involving minors in Michigan. A minor is a person under the age of eighteen. Under the law, a minor cannot act for himself or herself and therefore a competent adult must be appointed before a lawsuit is filed in court or a settlement can be received.</p>

<h2>If a lawsuit has not been filed in Circuit Court, any settlement is governed by the Probate rules.</h2>

<p>If the settlement is for less than $5,000.00, the settlement proceeds normally do not need to be held in a restricted account. The requirements of placing settlement proceeds in a restricted account may also be avoided where a structured settlement is used to protect the proceeds for a minor in a personal injury settlement.</p>

<p><strong>In Michigan, a minor and their parent cannot accept a settlement in a personal injury case without the court's approval.</strong></p>

<p>This provision is designed to protect the interests of minors who have been injured in an accident caused by negligence of a third party.</p>

<ul>
  <li>A judge can refuse a settlement if he or she feels that it provides inadequate compensation for the severity of the injuries or trauma sustained by the child.</li>
  <li>A judge may direct where the money awarded to a minor is held or invested so the money is not squandered by either the minor or their parent.</li>
</ul>
