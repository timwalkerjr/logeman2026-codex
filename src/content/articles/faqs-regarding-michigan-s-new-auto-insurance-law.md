<h2><strong>Understanding Michigan's New Auto Insurance Law</strong></h2>
<p>In June 2019, Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer signed a sweeping overhaul of the state's auto insurance laws. The new law, which took effect on July 2, 2020, represented the most significant change to Michigan's no-fault insurance system in decades. If you drive in Michigan, understanding these changes is critical to making informed decisions about your coverage and protecting your rights after an accident.</p>
<p>At <a href="/about-us/">Logeman & Iafrate, P.C.</a>, we've been helping Michigan accident victims navigate the complexities of the state's auto insurance system for over 45 years. Here are answers to the most common questions about the new law.</p>

<h2><strong>What Changed Under Michigan's New Auto Insurance Law?</strong></h2>
<p>The new law made several significant changes to Michigan's auto insurance system:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>PIP Coverage Choices:</strong> Drivers can now select from multiple Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage levels instead of being required to carry unlimited coverage.</li>
  <li><strong>Lawsuit Thresholds:</strong> Modified the requirements for filing lawsuits against at-fault drivers in serious injury cases.</li>
  <li><strong>Premium Reductions:</strong> Required insurance companies to reduce premiums to reflect the new coverage options.</li>
  <li><strong>Rate Filing Changes:</strong> Changed how insurance companies file rates with state regulators.</li>
  <li><strong>Medical Fee Schedules:</strong> Established fee schedules for medical services provided to accident victims.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>PIP Coverage Options Explained</strong></h2>
<p>Under the new law, drivers can choose from the following PIP coverage levels:</p>

<h3><strong>1. Unlimited PIP Coverage</strong></h3>
<p>This is the traditional Michigan no-fault coverage, providing unlimited medical benefits for accident-related injuries. This remains the default if you don't make an active selection during the transition period.</p>

<h3><strong>2. $500,000 PIP Coverage</strong></h3>
<p>A middle-ground option that provides up to $500,000 in medical benefits. This may be suitable for drivers with good health insurance who want meaningful protection at a lower premium.</p>

<h3><strong>3. $250,000 PIP Coverage</strong></h3>
<p>This option provides up to $250,000 in medical benefits. Drivers choosing this level must have valid health insurance that would cover auto accident injuries.</p>

<h3><strong>4. $50,000 PIP Coverage</strong></h3>
<p>The minimum PIP coverage level available, providing up to $50,000 in medical benefits. This option requires that you have valid health insurance coverage, and only drivers enrolled in Medicaid may choose this option with opt-out provisions.</p>

<h2><strong>Lawsuit Thresholds Under the New Law</strong></h2>
<p>One of the most significant changes in the new law relates to when you can sue an at-fault driver for non-economic damages like pain and suffering. The new law modified the lawsuit thresholds based on your PIP coverage level:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Unlimited or $500,000 PIP:</strong> Drivers retain broader lawsuit rights and can sue for non-economic damages if they suffer serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death.</li>
  <li><strong>$250,000 or $50,000 PIP:</strong> Drivers have more limited lawsuit rights, generally requiring more severe injury thresholds (like permanent serious disfigurement or death) before they can sue for pain and suffering.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>How the New Law Affects Insurance Premiums</strong></h2>
<p>Michigan historically had some of the highest auto insurance premiums in the nation. The new law was designed to reduce these costs by giving drivers more coverage choices. While individual savings vary based on factors like your chosen coverage level, location, driving history, and other rating factors, many Michigan drivers have seen meaningful reductions in their auto insurance premiums.</p>
<p>However, it's important to note that lower premiums come with trade-offs in coverage. Drivers who choose lower PIP coverage levels may face significant out-of-pocket expenses if they're seriously injured in an accident.</p>

<h2><strong>Medical Fee Schedules</strong></h2>
<p>The new law established fee schedules for medical services provided to accident victims. These fee schedules set maximum amounts that insurance companies must pay for various medical procedures and services. While intended to control costs, these fee schedules have been controversial and have raised concerns about access to care for seriously injured accident victims.</p>

<h2><strong>Special Considerations for Accident Victims</strong></h2>
<p>If you've been injured in a car accident in Michigan after July 2, 2020, the new law affects your rights in several important ways:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>PIP Benefits:</strong> Your available PIP benefits depend on the coverage level selected by the at-fault driver's (or your own) insurance policy.</li>
  <li><strong>Lawsuit Rights:</strong> Your ability to file a lawsuit for pain and suffering depends on the severity of your injuries and the PIP coverage levels involved.</li>
  <li><strong>Medical Treatment:</strong> Your medical providers may be subject to fee schedules that limit the amounts they can charge.</li>
  <li><strong>Time Limits:</strong> Strict deadlines still apply for filing claims and lawsuits, including the three-year statute of limitations for personal injury claims.</li>
</ul>
