<p>If you are involved in a <a href="/motor-vehicle-accidents-overview/car-accident-attorney-ann-arbor-mi/">car accident</a> in Michigan, you should use your phone camera to take photos after you have used it to call 9-1-1 or contacted the local police agency. The pictures you take at the accident scene can later help provide proof as to what occurred in the <a href="/">accident</a>. Photographs will be helpful if you later choose to <a href="/articles/should-i-hire-an-attorney-for-a-car-accident-personal-injury-lawyers-ann-arbor">pursue compensation for your injuries</a> or when establishing property damage claims. If you are unable to take photographs due to your <a href="/motor-vehicle-accidents-overview/drunk-driver-attorney-ann-arbor-mi/">injuries</a>, you should have friends or family members take photographs as soon as possible.</p>

<h2>Photographs of the Accident Scene Can Be an Important Step Toward Documenting Scene Evidence That Will Be Helpful to Prove Fault</h2>
<p>Photographs should be taken to show the position of cars, the point of impact, and relevant traffic signs, vehicle damage, and road conditions. This evidence can also help demonstrate the extent of the damage done to your car during the crash and that those damages occurred during the actual collision and not at some other unrelated time.</p>

<p><strong>Even if you are at fault for the accident, you should take photographs. These photos could document the actual damage to the involved vehicles so that you will not be held responsible for property damage you did not cause.</strong></p>

<p>You should also take photographs of your injuries. Photos can help you prove that the injuries you are suffering from occurred during the crash and can be used if the insurance company claims that the injuries were pre-existing or unrelated to the collision.</p>

<p><strong>Further, you should also obtain the following accident information:</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>Any witnesses and their names and contact information</li>
  <li>The license plate and insurance information of the other parties involved in the collision</li>
  <li>Make sure to inform the investigating police officer that you are injured if you feel pain even if you think the injuries are minor. The police officer should document this in the police report. Otherwise, if the officer indicates that you are not injured, the insurance company may later use this as a reason to deny your <a href="/motor-vehicle-accidents-overview/no-fault-claim-attorney-ann-arbor-mi/">personal injury claim</a>.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>In Michigan, police use the following injury codes:</strong></p>

<p><strong>K – Fatal Injury</strong></p>
<p>Any injury which results in death.</p>

<p><strong>A – Incapacitating Injury</strong></p>
<p>Any injury other than fatal which prevents normal activities and generally requires hospitalization.</p>

<p><strong>B – Non-Incapacitating Injury</strong></p>
<p>Any injury not incapacitating but evident to to others at the scene.</p>

<p><strong>C – Possible Injury</strong></p>
<p>No visible injury but complaint of pain or momentary unconsciousness.</p>

<p><strong>O – No Injury</strong></p>
<p>No indication of injury.</p>

<p>In summary, you should take pictures of anything and everything that might be relevant to your claim. There is no such thing as taking too many pictures, and in some cases, even photographs that initially seem unnecessary may end up providing the most important information.</p>
