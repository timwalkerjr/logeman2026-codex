<p>The moments after a car accident can be overwhelming, especially if you've been injured. Knowing what steps to take after a <a href="/practice-area/car-accidents/">car accident</a> involving personal injury can protect your health, preserve your legal rights, and significantly impact your ability to recover fair compensation. Follow this guide to ensure you're taking the right actions.</p>
<h2><strong>1. Ensure Safety and Call for Help</strong></h2>
<p>Your safety and the safety of others should be your first priority. If possible, move your vehicle out of traffic to a safe location. Turn on your hazard lights and check yourself and your passengers for injuries. If anyone is injured, call 911 immediately for emergency medical assistance.</p>
<p>Even if the accident seems minor, calling the police is important. A police report provides official documentation of the accident, which can be critical evidence for insurance claims and potential lawsuits.</p>
<h2><strong>2. Document the Accident Scene</strong></h2>
<p>If you are able, thoroughly document the accident scene before vehicles are moved or conditions change:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Take photos and videos of all vehicles involved, including damage and license plates</li>
  <li>Capture the overall scene, including road conditions, traffic signs, and weather</li>
  <li>Photograph any visible injuries you or your passengers sustained</li>
  <li>Note the position of the vehicles and any debris</li>
  <li>Record the time, date, and location of the accident</li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>3. Exchange Information With Other Parties</strong></h2>
<p>Collect the following information from all other drivers involved:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Names, addresses, and phone numbers</li>
  <li>Driver's license numbers</li>
  <li>License plate numbers</li>
  <li>Insurance company names and policy numbers</li>
  <li>Vehicle make, model, and year</li>
</ul>
<p>Also collect contact information from any witnesses to the accident. Be sure to obtain the responding officer's name and badge number, and ask how to obtain a copy of the police report.</p>
<h2><strong>4. Seek Immediate Medical Attention</strong></h2>
<p>Even if you feel fine after the accident, seek medical attention as soon as possible. Some serious injuries, such as concussions, internal bleeding, or soft tissue damage, may not show symptoms for hours or even days after the accident. Prompt medical care:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Identifies injuries you may not be aware of</li>
  <li>Creates a medical record linking your injuries to the accident</li>
  <li>Strengthens any future legal claims</li>
  <li>Prevents injuries from worsening due to delayed treatment</li>
</ul>
<p>Follow all treatment recommendations from your healthcare providers and attend all follow-up appointments. Gaps in treatment can be used by insurance companies to argue that your injuries are not as serious as you claim.</p>
<h2><strong>5. Notify Your Insurance Company</strong></h2>
<p>Report the accident to your insurance company promptly, as most policies require timely notification. However, be careful about what you say. Stick to the basic facts of the accident and avoid speculation, opinions, or detailed statements about your injuries. Do not admit fault or discuss the accident in detail until you've spoken with an attorney.</p>
<p>Avoid giving a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company without legal representation. Their adjusters are trained to obtain statements that can be used to reduce or deny your claim.</p>
<h2><strong>6. Preserve Evidence and Documentation</strong></h2>
<p>Begin keeping a file of all documents related to the accident:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Medical records and bills</li>
  <li>Pay stubs showing lost wages</li>
  <li>Receipts for out-of-pocket expenses</li>
  <li>Insurance correspondence</li>
  <li>Police report and accident scene photos</li>
  <li>Names and contact information for witnesses</li>
  <li>A journal documenting your recovery, pain levels, and how injuries affect your daily life</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep all evidence related to your accident organized and accessible. This documentation will be invaluable when pursuing your claim.</p>
<h2><strong>7. Be Cautious on Social Media</strong></h2>
<p>Avoid posting about the accident or your injuries on social media. Insurance companies and defense attorneys routinely monitor claimants' social media accounts for posts that can be used against them. Even innocent posts about activities or comments about how you're feeling can be misrepresented to undermine your claim.</p>
<h2><strong>8. Consult With an Experienced Car Accident Attorney</strong></h2>
<p>Before accepting any settlement offer or making detailed statements to insurance companies, consult with an experienced <a href="/practice-area/car-accidents/">Michigan car accident attorney</a>. An attorney can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Evaluate the strength of your claim</li>
  <li>Handle communications with insurance companies</li>
  <li>Ensure all deadlines are met</li>
  <li>Negotiate for the full compensation you deserve</li>
  <li>Pursue litigation if a fair settlement cannot be reached</li>
</ul>
<p>Most personal injury attorneys, including our firm, offer free consultations and work on a contingency fee basis, meaning you pay nothing unless we win your case.</p>
<h2><strong>9. Don't Accept the First Settlement Offer</strong></h2>
<p>Insurance companies often make quick settlement offers that are far less than what your claim is actually worth. These early offers are typically made before the full extent of your injuries is known and before you've had time to consult with an attorney. Never accept a settlement without first having it reviewed by a lawyer.</p>
