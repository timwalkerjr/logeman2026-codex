<h2><strong>Protect Your Michigan Personal Injury Claim: Mistakes to Avoid</strong></h2>
<p>After suffering an injury in an accident, the choices you make in the days, weeks, and months that follow can have a profound impact on your ability to recover fair compensation. Unfortunately, many injury victims make critical mistakes that weaken their claims, reduce their settlements, or even eliminate their right to compensation altogether.</p>
<p>At <a href="/about-us/">Logeman & Iafrate, P.C.</a>, we've spent over 45 years helping injury victims in Ann Arbor and throughout Michigan recover the compensation they deserve. We've seen firsthand how simple mistakes can devastate an otherwise strong case. Here's what you need to know to protect your rights.</p>

<h2><strong>Mistake #1: Delaying Medical Treatment</strong></h2>
<p>One of the most damaging mistakes you can make after an accident is delaying medical treatment. Many people downplay their injuries, hoping they'll feel better with rest. Others worry about the cost of medical care or simply don't want to deal with doctors. However, delaying treatment can seriously harm both your health and your legal claim.</p>
<p><strong>How it hurts your case:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>Insurance companies will argue that your injuries aren't as severe as you claim, or that they were caused by something other than the accident.</li>
  <li>A gap in treatment makes it harder for your medical expert to connect your injuries to the accident.</li>
  <li>You may lose the right to claim compensation for the full extent of your injuries.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong> Seek medical attention immediately after the accident, even if you feel fine. Follow all treatment recommendations, attend every appointment, and follow through with prescribed therapies. Document everything.</p>

<h2><strong>Mistake #2: Giving a Recorded Statement to the Insurance Company</strong></h2>
<p>Shortly after your accident, you will likely receive a call from an insurance adjuster asking for a recorded statement. The adjuster may sound friendly and sympathetic, but their job is to minimize your claim. Anything you say can be twisted and used against you.</p>
<p><strong>How it hurts your case:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>Adjusters use leading questions designed to elicit responses that minimize your injuries or suggest you were at fault.</li>
  <li>Simple statements like "I'm feeling better" can be used to argue that your injuries are not serious.</li>
  <li>Even minor inconsistencies in your story can be used to challenge your credibility.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong> Politely decline to give a recorded statement until you've consulted with an attorney. Refer all communications from the insurance company to your lawyer. A skilled personal injury attorney knows how to handle insurance adjusters and can protect you from making damaging statements.</p>

<h2><strong>Mistake #3: Posting About Your Accident on Social Media</strong></h2>
<p>In today's digital world, posting about your life on social media is second nature. However, after an accident, your social media activity can come back to haunt you. Insurance companies and defense attorneys routinely monitor the social media accounts of injury victims.</p>
<p><strong>How it hurts your case:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>A photo of you at a family gathering can be used to argue that you're not really injured.</li>
  <li>Comments about feeling "fine" or "great" can be used to minimize your pain and suffering.</li>
  <li>Even old photos or posts can be taken out of context.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong> Stop posting on social media entirely while your case is pending. If you must use social media, set your accounts to private and avoid any posts about your accident, injuries, or activities. Better yet, deactivate your accounts until your case is resolved.</p>

<h2><strong>Mistake #4: Settling Too Quickly</strong></h2>
<p>Insurance companies often make quick settlement offers hoping to close out claims before the full extent of your injuries becomes clear. While these offers may seem tempting, especially when you're facing mounting medical bills and lost wages, accepting an early settlement is almost always a mistake.</p>
<p><strong>How it hurts your case:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>You may not yet know the full extent of your injuries or how they will affect you in the future.</li>
  <li>Once you accept a settlement, you typically waive your right to seek additional compensation.</li>
  <li>Early settlement offers are almost always significantly lower than what your case is actually worth.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong> Wait until you have reached maximum medical improvement (MMI) before considering settlement. Consult with an experienced personal injury attorney who can evaluate your case and negotiate a fair settlement on your behalf. A good attorney will know when to settle and when to push for more.</p>

<h2><strong>Mistake #5: Not Hiring an Experienced Attorney</strong></h2>
<p>Many injury victims try to handle their claims on their own, thinking they'll save money on attorney fees. Others hire an attorney based solely on a flashy advertisement, without doing their research. Both approaches can lead to disappointing results.</p>
<p><strong>How it hurts your case:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>Insurance companies take cases more seriously when an experienced attorney is involved.</li>
  <li>Without legal expertise, you may miss critical deadlines or fail to preserve important evidence.</li>
  <li>You may not understand the full value of your claim, leading to an inadequate settlement.</li>
  <li>Handling complex legal procedures without an attorney often leads to costly mistakes.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong> Hire an experienced personal injury attorney with a proven track record. Look for an attorney who specializes in personal injury law, has years of experience handling cases similar to yours, and has trial experience. Most importantly, hire an attorney who will personally handle your case, not pass it off to a paralegal or associate.</p>

<h2><strong>Additional Mistakes That Can Weaken Your Claim</strong></h2>
<p>In addition to the five major mistakes above, several other errors can damage your personal injury claim:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Failing to document the accident scene:</strong> Take photos and videos immediately after the accident.</li>
  <li><strong>Not following your doctor's orders:</strong> Missing appointments or skipping prescribed treatments can hurt your case.</li>
  <li><strong>Discussing your case with others:</strong> Avoid talking about your case with anyone other than your attorney.</li>
  <li><strong>Missing important deadlines:</strong> Michigan's statute of limitations and no-fault insurance deadlines are strict.</li>
  <li><strong>Signing documents without legal review:</strong> Never sign anything from the insurance company without consulting your attorney.</li>
</ul>
