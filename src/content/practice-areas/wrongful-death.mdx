<h3><strong>Understanding Michigan's Wrongful Death Act</strong></h3>
<p>Under Michigan's Wrongful Death Act (MCL 600.2922), surviving family members can file a lawsuit when a death is caused by wrongful act, neglect, or fault—essentially any situation where the deceased could have filed a personal injury lawsuit if they had survived.</p>
<p>The lawsuit must be filed by the personal representative (executor) of the deceased's estate. Family members who may be entitled to damages include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Spouse</li>
  <li>Children and stepchildren</li>
  <li>Parents</li>
  <li>Grandparents</li>
  <li>Siblings</li>
  <li>Those financially dependent on the deceased</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Compensation Available in Wrongful Death Cases</strong></h3>
<p>Michigan law allows recovery for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Medical, hospital, funeral, and burial expenses</li>
  <li>Pain and suffering experienced by the deceased between injury and death</li>
  <li>Loss of financial support and inheritance</li>
  <li>Loss of society and companionship</li>
</ul>
<p>The three-year statute of limitations for wrongful death claims begins from the date of death. Prompt action is essential to preserve evidence and protect your family's rights.</p>
