<h2>Types of Product Defects Recognized Under Michigan Law</h2>

<p>Michigan recognizes three main categories of product defects:</p>

<h3>Design Defects</h3>

<p>A product has a design defect when the product's very design makes it unreasonably dangerous, even if it's manufactured exactly as designed. Examples include vehicles with unstable designs that rollover easily, power tools without safety guards, and electronic devices prone to overheating and catching fire. To prove a design defect, we typically work with engineering and industry experts who can demonstrate that a safer, cost-effective alternative design existed at the time the product was made.</p>

<h3>Manufacturing Defects</h3>

<p>Manufacturing defects occur when a product departs from its intended design during production. These defects affect only some units of a product line, while others may be safe. Examples include contaminated foods, improperly welded components, missing parts, and quality control failures that cause specific products to fail. We trace these defects back to specific production runs, batches, or facilities.</p>

<h3>Marketing Defects (Failure to Warn)</h3>

<p>Even products that are properly designed and manufactured can be defective if they lack adequate warnings about known risks. Pharmaceutical drugs, medical devices, chemicals, and consumer products must include warnings about foreseeable dangers. When manufacturers know—or should know—about serious risks and fail to provide adequate warnings, they can be held liable for injuries.</p>

<h2>Michigan's Product Liability Statute (MCL 600.2946)</h2>

<p>Michigan's product liability statute, MCL 600.2946, establishes the framework for product liability actions in the state. Key provisions include:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A product is defective when it is not reasonably safe for its intended use</li>
  <li>Manufacturers and sellers can be held liable for injuries caused by defective products</li>
  <li>The statute recognizes claims based on design defects, manufacturing defects, and failure to warn</li>
  <li>The statute of limitations is three years from injury, with a 15-year statute of repose</li>
  <li>The "consumer contemplation test" considers what an ordinary consumer would expect from the product</li>
</ul>

<h3>Senate Bill 410 and Pharmaceutical Immunity</h3>

<p>In 2023, Michigan enacted Senate Bill 410, which modified product liability rules for pharmaceutical drugs and medical devices. The law provides certain defenses to manufacturers of FDA-approved products but doesn't create absolute immunity. Our attorneys stay current with these evolving rules and understand how they affect your case.</p>

<h2>Who Can Be Held Liable for a Defective Product?</h2>

<p>Michigan's product liability laws allow claims against multiple parties in the distribution chain:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Manufacturers</strong>—the company that designed, assembled, or produced the product</li>
  <li><strong>Component part manufacturers</strong>—makers of specific parts that were defective and caused the harm</li>
  <li><strong>Distributors and wholesalers</strong>—companies that handled the product between manufacturer and retailer</li>
  <li><strong>Retailers</strong>—the store or business that sold the product directly to the consumer</li>
  <li><strong>Lessors</strong>—companies that lease or rent products to consumers</li>
</ul>

<p>We identify all potentially liable parties to maximize your recovery.</p>

<h2>Compensation Available for Calhoun County Product Liability Injuries</h2>

<p>Victims of defective products can pursue several types of damages:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Economic damages:</strong> Medical expenses (past and future), lost wages, diminished earning capacity, rehabilitation costs, and out-of-pocket expenses</li>
  <li><strong>Non-economic damages:</strong> Pain and suffering, emotional distress, mental anguish, loss of enjoyment of life, and loss of consortium</li>
  <li><strong>Punitive damages:</strong> In cases involving particularly reckless or malicious conduct, Michigan courts may award punitive damages to punish the manufacturer and deter similar misconduct</li>
  <li><strong>Wrongful death damages:</strong> When a defective product causes death, surviving family members can pursue wrongful death claims</li>
</ul>

<p>The compensation available depends on the severity of your injuries and the specific facts of your case. Catastrophic injuries can result in millions of dollars in damages.</p>
