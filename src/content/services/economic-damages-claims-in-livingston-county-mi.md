<h2>What Are Economic Damages?</h2>

<p>Economic damages compensate for objectively measurable financial losses caused by another person's negligence. Unlike non-economic damages for pain and suffering, economic damages address concrete monetary harm with documentation to support every dollar claimed.</p>

<p>In <a href="/">Michigan personal injury cases</a>, economic damages typically include:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Medical expenses.</strong> Past and future costs for emergency care, surgery, hospitalization, doctor visits, prescription medications, physical therapy, rehabilitation, medical equipment, and all necessary treatment related to your injuries.</li>
  <li><strong>Lost income.</strong> Wages lost while recovering from injuries, including sick days, vacation time used, and unpaid leave. Documentation from employers establishes these losses with precision.</li>
  <li><strong>Lost earning capacity.</strong> When injuries prevent you from returning to your former work or limit your ability to earn at the same level, you can recover the difference between what you would have earned and what you can now earn for the rest of your working life.</li>
  <li><strong>Household services.</strong> Compensation for services you can no longer perform—lawn care, housekeeping, child care, home maintenance—that you must now pay others to handle.</li>
  <li><strong>Property damage.</strong> Vehicle repair or replacement costs in auto accidents, along with damage to personal property.</li>
  <li><strong>Future care costs.</strong> For catastrophic injuries requiring lifetime care, economic damages include projected costs for ongoing medical treatment, attendant care, adaptive equipment, and home modifications calculated over your life expectancy.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Economic Damages Under Michigan's No-Fault System</h2>

<p>Livingston County auto accident victims face unique challenges due to Michigan's no-fault insurance system. Your own auto insurance provides Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits covering medical expenses, wage loss (up to 85% of gross income), and replacement services—regardless of who caused the crash.</p>

<p>However, the 2019-2020 no-fault reforms changed coverage dramatically. Depending on your policy choice, your PIP medical benefits may be capped at $50,000, $250,000, $500,000, or remain unlimited. When your economic losses exceed these caps, third-party claims against the at-fault driver become essential.</p>

<p>To pursue excess economic damages beyond PIP coverage, your injuries must meet Michigan's "serious impairment of body function" threshold. Our attorneys have extensive experience proving threshold injuries and maximizing third-party recoveries for Livingston County clients.</p>

<h2>Proving Economic Damages in Livingston County</h2>

<p>Insurance companies routinely challenge economic damage claims, arguing that treatment is unnecessary, losses are exaggerated, or injuries stem from pre-existing conditions. Successfully recovering full economic damages requires comprehensive documentation and expert testimony.</p>

<p>Our attorneys work with economic experts, life care planners, and vocational specialists to establish:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Medical necessity.</strong> Expert testimony from treating physicians demonstrating that all past and future medical expenses directly relate to the accident and represent appropriate, necessary care.</li>
  <li><strong>Lifetime care costs.</strong> Life care planners evaluate catastrophic injuries to project future medical expenses, attendant care needs, equipment costs, and home modifications over your life expectancy.</li>
  <li><strong>Lost earning capacity.</strong> Vocational experts analyze your work history, education, skills, physical limitations, and labor market conditions to calculate reduced earning capacity over your remaining work life.</li>
  <li><strong>Future wage loss.</strong> Economists project career earnings trajectories, accounting for promotions, raises, benefits, and inflation to demonstrate the full financial impact of your injuries.</li>
</ul>
