<h2><strong>Michigan's Distracted Driving Laws</strong></h2>
<p>Michigan law prohibits texting while driving. Under MCL 257.602b, it's illegal to manually read, type, or send a text message while operating a vehicle. Violations carry fines, but more importantly, they establish negligence in personal injury cases. When we can prove the at-fault driver was texting or otherwise distracted at the time of your crash, it strengthens your claim significantly.</p>
<p>However, the law doesn't stop all dangerous behavior. Drivers still eat behind the wheel, adjust GPS devices, discipline children in the backseat, and engage in countless other distractions. Whether the distraction violates a specific statute or simply represents careless driving, our attorneys build the evidence needed to prove negligence and maximize your recovery.</p>

<h2><strong>Common Types of Distracted Driving in Wayne County</strong></h2>
<p>Distraction takes many forms, but they all share the same result—a driver who isn't focused on the road and the safety of others. Common distractions we see in Wayne County accident cases include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Texting and cell phone use.</strong> Reading or sending messages, checking social media, browsing the internet, and talking on handheld phones.</li>
  <li><strong>Eating and drinking.</strong> Drivers who handle food, unwrap items, or reach for beverages lose control and reaction time.</li>
  <li><strong>Adjusting controls.</strong> Changing radio stations, programming GPS devices, and adjusting climate controls all take attention from the road.</li>
  <li><strong>Grooming.</strong> Applying makeup, shaving, fixing hair, and similar activities behind the wheel are shockingly common.</li>
  <li><strong>Interacting with passengers.</strong> Turning to talk, disciplining children, or reaching into the backseat creates dangerous moments.</li>
  <li><strong>Daydreaming.</strong> Even when hands are on the wheel, a mind that wanders isn't focused on hazards ahead.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>Compensation Available in Wayne County Distracted Driving Cases</strong></h2>
<p>Michigan's no-fault auto insurance system provides Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits for medical expenses, lost wages, and replacement services—regardless of who caused the crash. However, if your injuries meet Michigan's serious impairment of body function threshold, you can also pursue a third-party claim against the distracted driver for additional damages including:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pain and suffering.</strong> Compensation for physical pain and emotional distress caused by your injuries.</li>
  <li><strong>Excess economic damages.</strong> Medical expenses and wage losses beyond what your PIP coverage provides.</li>
  <li><strong>Permanent disability.</strong> Compensation for lasting impairments that affect your quality of life.</li>
  <li><strong>Loss of consortium.</strong> Damages for the impact on your relationship with your spouse.</li>
</ul>
<p>Successfully recovering these damages requires proving that the at-fault driver was negligent and that their distraction caused the crash. Our attorneys investigate every detail, obtain phone records when necessary, interview witnesses, and work with accident reconstruction experts to build the strongest possible case.</p>

<h2><strong>Proving Distracted Driving in Your Wayne County Case</strong></h2>
<p>Insurance companies defend distracted driving claims aggressively. They'll argue the driver wasn't actually distracted, that other factors caused the crash, or that your injuries aren't as serious as you claim. We've heard every defense tactic, and we know how to defeat them.</p>
<p>Building a strong case requires gathering evidence quickly before it disappears. Our investigation includes:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Cell phone records.</strong> We subpoena phone records to prove the driver was texting or making calls at the time of impact.</li>
  <li><strong>Witness statements.</strong> We interview everyone who saw the crash to establish what the driver was doing before impact.</li>
  <li><strong>Police reports.</strong> We obtain official accident reports that may document admissions or citations for distracted driving.</li>
  <li><strong>Accident reconstruction.</strong> We work with experts who analyze crash dynamics, skid marks, and vehicle damage to prove negligence.</li>
  <li><strong>Electronic data.</strong> Modern vehicles contain event data recorders that capture speed, braking, and other critical information.</li>
</ul>
