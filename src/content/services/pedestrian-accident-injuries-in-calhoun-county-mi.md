<h2>Understanding Michigan's No-Fault System for Pedestrian Accidents</h2>

<p>Michigan's no-fault insurance system applies to pedestrian accidents, but the coverage situation can be complex. Pedestrians injured by motor vehicles are entitled to Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits—but the source of those benefits depends on your specific circumstances.</p>

<p><strong>If you own a vehicle with no-fault insurance:</strong> Your own auto policy provides PIP benefits for medical expenses, wage loss, and attendant care—regardless of who caused the accident.</p>

<p><strong>If you live with a relative who has auto insurance:</strong> That household policy typically provides coverage for your pedestrian injuries.</p>

<p><strong>If you have no connection to auto insurance:</strong> The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan provides PIP benefits through a state-assigned insurer.</p>

<p>The 2019-2020 no-fault reforms significantly changed the landscape. Depending on your coverage level, PIP medical benefits may be capped at $50,000, $250,000, $500,000, or remain unlimited. Understanding your coverage—or identifying which policy applies—is critical to securing the medical care you need.</p>

<h2>Third-Party Claims Against Negligent Drivers</h2>

<p>Beyond no-fault benefits, injured pedestrians can pursue compensation directly from the at-fault driver if injuries meet Michigan's "serious impairment of body function" threshold. This requires proving an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life.</p>

<p>Meeting this threshold allows you to recover damages that no-fault benefits don't cover:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Pain and suffering</li>
  <li>Emotional distress and psychological trauma</li>
  <li>Excess medical expenses beyond PIP limits</li>
  <li>Full lost wages and diminished earning capacity</li>
  <li>Permanent disability and disfigurement</li>
</ul>

<p>Our attorneys have extensive experience proving threshold injuries and maximizing third-party recoveries for pedestrian accident victims in Calhoun County.</p>

<h2>Injuries Commonly Suffered by Pedestrian Accident Victims</h2>

<p>The human body is no match for a multi-ton vehicle. Pedestrian accidents frequently result in severe, life-changing injuries:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Traumatic brain injuries (TBI):</strong> Concussions, skull fractures, and permanent cognitive impairment from head impact</li>
  <li><strong>Spinal cord injuries:</strong> Paralysis, paraplegia, or quadriplegia requiring lifetime care</li>
  <li><strong>Broken bones:</strong> Fractures to legs, arms, hips, and pelvis that may require surgery and extended rehabilitation</li>
  <li><strong>Internal injuries:</strong> Organ damage and internal bleeding that can be life-threatening</li>
  <li><strong>Severe lacerations and road rash:</strong> Deep cuts requiring stitches, skin grafts, and leaving permanent scars</li>
  <li><strong>Amputations:</strong> Loss of limbs from severe trauma</li>
  <li><strong>Soft tissue injuries:</strong> Ligament tears, muscle damage, and nerve injuries</li>
</ul>

<p>These injuries often require extensive hospitalization, multiple surgeries, prolonged rehabilitation, and ongoing medical care. The financial burden can be overwhelming—making full compensation essential.</p>
