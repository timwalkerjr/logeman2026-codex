<h2>Understanding Paralysis and Its Causes</h2>

<p>Paralysis occurs when nerve damage prevents signals from reaching muscles, resulting in loss of movement and sensation. The severity and location of paralysis depend on where the spinal cord or brain injury occurred:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Paraplegia:</strong> Paralysis affecting the lower body, legs, and pelvic organs, typically resulting from thoracic or lumbar spinal cord injuries</li>
  <li><strong>Quadriplegia (Tetraplegia):</strong> Paralysis affecting all four limbs and the torso, caused by cervical spine injuries in the neck region</li>
  <li><strong>Hemiplegia:</strong> Paralysis affecting one side of the body, often resulting from traumatic brain injury or stroke</li>
  <li><strong>Complete paralysis:</strong> Total loss of sensation and motor function below the injury site</li>
  <li><strong>Incomplete paralysis:</strong> Partial preservation of some sensation or movement below the injury level</li>
</ul>

<p>Common causes of paralysis injuries in Washtenaw County include motor vehicle accidents on US-23 and I-94, construction site falls, medical negligence during surgery, premises liability incidents, and sports or recreational accidents. When another party's negligence causes your paralysis, Michigan law provides pathways to recovery.</p>

<h2>Compensation Sources for Washtenaw County Paralysis Victims</h2>

<p>Michigan's legal system provides multiple potential sources of compensation depending on how your paralysis occurred:</p>

<h3>Auto Accident Paralysis Claims</h3>
<p>If a car, truck, or motorcycle accident on Washtenaw County roads caused your paralysis, you're entitled to Michigan no-fault Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits covering medical expenses and attendant care. The 2019-2020 reforms created different coverage levels, making it critical to understand your policy limits.</p>

<p>Additionally, if your injuries meet Michigan's serious impairment threshold—which paralysis undoubtedly does—you can pursue a third-party lawsuit against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, excess medical costs, and full economic damages beyond PIP limits.</p>

<h3>Workers' Compensation and Third-Party Claims</h3>
<p>Construction workers, manufacturing employees, and other Washtenaw County workers paralyzed on the job qualify for workers' compensation benefits. However, third-party claims against negligent contractors, equipment manufacturers, or property owners can provide additional compensation for pain and suffering that workers' compensation doesn't address.</p>

<h3>Medical Malpractice</h3>
<p>When paralysis results from surgical errors, anesthesia mistakes, birth injuries, or delayed diagnosis at University of Michigan Hospital or other Washtenaw County medical facilities, medical malpractice claims can hold negligent providers accountable. These cases require extensive expert testimony but can result in substantial compensation.</p>

<h3>Premises Liability</h3>
<p>Property owners who fail to maintain safe conditions—leading to falls from heights, slip and falls on ice, or other dangerous conditions—may be liable when their negligence causes paralysis. Michigan's premises liability law requires property owners to address known hazards and warn visitors of dangers.</p>
