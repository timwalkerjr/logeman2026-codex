<h2>Michigan's Distracted Driving Laws</h2>

<p>Michigan has enacted laws to combat distracted driving:</p>

<p><strong>Kelsey's Law:</strong> Prohibits drivers with Level 1 or Level 2 graduated licenses from using a cell phone while driving, with limited exceptions.</p>

<p><strong>Texting ban:</strong> Michigan prohibits texting while driving for all motorists, with primary enforcement meaning officers can pull drivers over solely for this violation.</p>

<p>Violations of these laws provide strong evidence of negligence in accident cases, though you can pursue claims against distracted drivers regardless of whether they were cited. Our attorneys investigate whether distracted drivers violated Michigan law and use these violations to strengthen your claim.</p>

  <li><strong>Failure to yield:</strong> Missing right-of-way signs and failing to yield at intersections</li>

  <li><strong>Highway speed variations:</strong> Sudden braking or speed changes cause chain-reaction crashes</li>

</ul>

<p>The injuries from these crashes are often catastrophic because distracted drivers don't take evasive action before impact.</p>

<h2>Recovering Compensation After a Livingston County Distracted Driving Accident</h2>

<p>Michigan's no-fault insurance system provides benefits regardless of fault, but coverage depends on your PIP policy limits:</p>

<p><strong>No-fault PIP benefits:</strong> Your own auto insurance covers medical expenses up to your chosen coverage level ($50,000, $250,000, $500,000, or unlimited), 85% of lost wages, and replacement services for household tasks you cannot perform.</p>

<p><strong>Third-party claims:</strong> If your injuries meet Michigan's "serious impairment of body function" threshold, you can sue the distracted driver for damages beyond no-fault benefits.</p>

<p><strong>Employer liability:</strong> If the distracted driver was working, their employer may share responsibility.</p>

<p>Third-party claims allow recovery of pain and suffering, full wage loss, excess medical costs, lost earning capacity, and loss of enjoyment of life.</p>

<h2>Proving Distracted Driving</h2>

<p>Proving the other driver was distracted requires thorough investigation and compelling evidence. Our attorneys pursue every available avenue:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Cell phone records:</strong> Subpoenaing phone records showing calls, texts, and data usage at the time of the crash</li>
  <li><strong>Vehicle data:</strong> Obtaining event data recorder (EDR) downloads from the vehicle</li>
  <li><strong>Traffic camera footage:</strong> Securing video from traffic cameras, business security cameras, and dashcams</li>
  <li><strong>Witness testimony:</strong> Interviewing witnesses who saw the driver distracted</li>
  <li><strong>Driver admissions:</strong> Statements the driver made about texting, talking, or other distractions</li>
  <li><strong>Social media activity:</strong> Showing posts, messages, or app activity at the time of the crash</li>
  <li><strong>Accident reconstruction:</strong> Using experts to demonstrate the driver wasn't paying attention</li>
</ul>

<p>Cell phone records often provide the strongest evidence. Phone companies retain these records for varying periods, so prompt legal action is critical to preserve this evidence.</p>
