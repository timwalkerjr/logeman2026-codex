<h2>Why Construction Sites Are Dangerous in Calhoun County</h2>

<p>Construction work involves heavy machinery, elevated work areas, electrical systems, and powerful tools that create serious injury risks. Despite OSHA regulations designed to protect workers, preventable accidents continue to occur at alarming rates. Common hazards on Calhoun County job sites include:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Falls from heights</strong>—the leading cause of construction fatalities, often from scaffolding, ladders, roofs, and unprotected edges</li>
  <li><strong>Scaffolding collapses</strong> due to improper assembly, inadequate bracing, or exceeding load capacity</li>
  <li><strong>Crane and rigging failures</strong> causing crushing injuries and struck-by accidents</li>
  <li><strong>Electrical accidents</strong> from contact with overhead power lines or improperly grounded equipment</li>
  <li><strong>Trench and excavation collapses</strong> with inadequate shoring or protective systems</li>
  <li><strong>Struck-by accidents</strong> from falling tools, materials, or heavy equipment</li>
  <li><strong>Caught-between accidents</strong> involving machinery, vehicles, or collapsing structures</li>
  <li><strong>Defective equipment</strong> lacking proper safety guards or warning mechanisms</li>
  <li><strong>Inadequate safety training</strong> and failure to provide required personal protective equipment</li>
</ul>

<p>Many construction accidents result from preventable safety violations, inadequate training, or cost-cutting measures that put workers at risk.</p>

<h2>Common Construction Accident Injuries</h2>

<p>The force and nature of construction accidents frequently results in severe, life-altering injuries:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Traumatic brain injuries and concussions from falls or struck-by accidents</li>
  <li>Spinal cord damage leading to partial or complete paralysis</li>
  <li>Amputations from machinery, power tools, or crushing accidents</li>
  <li>Multiple fractures including compound and comminuted fractures</li>
  <li>Severe burns from electrical accidents or chemical exposure</li>
  <li>Internal organ damage and internal bleeding</li>
  <li>Crush injuries and compartment syndrome</li>
  <li>Occupational diseases from toxic exposure</li>
  <li>Permanent hearing loss from loud equipment</li>
  <li>Wrongful death from fatal construction accidents</li>
</ul>

<p>These injuries often require extensive surgery, long-term rehabilitation, and may result in permanent disabilities preventing return to construction work.</p>

<h2>Workers' Compensation for Construction Workers</h2>

<p>Michigan workers' compensation provides benefits regardless of who caused the accident, but the system has significant limitations:</p>

<p><strong>Medical care:</strong> Workers' comp covers reasonable and necessary medical treatment for your work-related injury. However, insurers frequently dispute treatment plans and authorize only minimum treatment.</p>

<p><strong>Wage loss benefits:</strong> Workers' compensation typically pays approximately 80% of your average weekly wage, subject to state maximums. This often falls far short of your actual earnings.</p>

<p><strong>Specific loss benefits:</strong> Set compensation amounts for permanent loss of use of hands, arms, feet, legs, or eyes.</p>

<p><strong>Vocational rehabilitation:</strong> Limited retraining benefits if you cannot return to your previous work.</p>

<p><strong>Death benefits:</strong> Surviving to dependents if a worker dies from a construction accident.</p>

<p>Workers' compensation does NOT cover pain and suffering, full wage loss, or punitive damages—making third-party claims crucial for full recovery.</p>

<h2>Third-Party Liability in Construction Accidents</h2>

<p>Beyond workers' compensation, injured construction workers can often file lawsuits against other parties whose negligence contributed to their injuries:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>General contractors</strong> for failing to maintain safe job sites, coordinate subcontractors, or enforce safety standards</li>
  <li><strong>Subcontractors</strong> whose negligent work created hazardous conditions</li>
  <li><strong>Property owners</strong> who failed to address known dangers on their property</li>
  <li><strong>Equipment manufacturers</strong> for defective machinery, tools, or safety devices</li>
  <li><strong>Engineers and architects</strong> for defective designs or specifications</li>
  <li><strong>Maintenance companies</strong> who failed to properly service equipment</li>
  <li><strong>Other contractors</strong> whose actions endangered workers on shared sites</li>
</ul>

<p>Third-party claims allow recovery of damages unavailable through workers' comp, including pain and suffering, full wage loss, and diminished earning capacity.</p>

<h2>OSHA Violations and Construction Accidents</h2>

<p>The Occupational Safety and Health Administration sets standards designed to protect construction workers. When employers or contractors violate OSHA regulations and workers are injured, those violations can serve as powerful evidence of negligence in third-party claims.</p>

<p>Common OSHA violations contributing to construction accidents include failure to provide fall protection, inadequate scaffolding, improper use of ladders, lack of protective equipment, insufficient training, and failure to follow crane and rigging safety standards. Our attorneys work with safety experts to identify all violations that contributed to your accident.</p>
