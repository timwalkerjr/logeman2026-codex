<h2><strong>Michigan's No-Fault Insurance System in Washtenaw County</strong></h2>
<p>Michigan's no-fault insurance law requires all auto policies to include Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage. After a car accident anywhere in Michigan—including Washtenaw County—your own insurance company pays for your medical care, lost wages, and other accident-related expenses, regardless of who caused the crash. The system was designed to ensure injured victims receive prompt benefits without having to prove fault.</p>
<p>The no-fault system has undergone significant changes in recent years, with reforms affecting what coverage levels are available and how benefits are paid. The rules that apply to your claim depend on when your policy was issued and what coverage level you chose. Our attorneys review your specific policy to determine exactly what benefits you're entitled to receive.</p>

<h2><strong>Types of PIP Benefits Available</strong></h2>
<p>PIP benefits are designed to cover the costs you face after a car accident. Available benefits typically include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Medical expenses.</strong> All reasonable and necessary medical treatment related to your injuries, including emergency care, hospital stays, doctor visits, surgery, physical therapy, medications, and medical equipment.</li>
  <li><strong>Lost wages.</strong> Up to 85% of your gross income for up to three years following the accident, with monthly maximums based on your coverage level.</li>
  <li><strong>Replacement services.</strong> Payment for household services you can no longer perform due to your injuries, such as cleaning, lawn care, snow removal, and cooking.</li>
  <li><strong>Attendant care.</strong> Nursing or caregiving services if your injuries require help with daily activities.</li>
  <li><strong>Rehabilitation.</strong> Vocational rehabilitation and occupational therapy to help you return to work and daily life.</li>
  <li><strong>Mileage reimbursement.</strong> Compensation for travel to and from medical appointments.</li>
</ul>
<p>Our attorneys identify all available benefits and pursue them aggressively on your behalf.</p>

<h2><strong>How to File a PIP Claim in Washtenaw County</strong></h2>
<p>To preserve your rights to PIP benefits, notify your insurance company as soon as possible after the accident—ideally within 24 to 48 hours. Most policies require written notice within 30 days. Once you've reported the accident, you'll need to:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Complete the application for benefits provided by your insurance company</li>
  <li>Submit medical records and treatment plans from your providers</li>
  <li>Provide documentation of lost wages (pay stubs, tax returns, employer letters)</li>
  <li>Submit receipts for any replacement services you've paid for</li>
  <li>Respond promptly to any requests for additional information</li>
</ol>
<p>Michigan law requires insurance companies to pay PIP claims within specific timeframes. If your insurer fails to pay or denies your claim, you have the right to file a lawsuit. Our attorneys handle every aspect of the claims process, from initial filing through litigation when necessary.</p>

<h2><strong>Common PIP Claim Disputes</strong></h2>
<p>Insurance companies use various tactics to deny or minimize PIP claims. Common disputes we resolve include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Treatment not reasonable or necessary.</strong> Insurers argue that ongoing physical therapy, specialist visits, or certain procedures exceed appropriate care.</li>
  <li><strong>Pre-existing conditions.</strong> Insurance companies try to attribute injuries to prior medical issues rather than the accident.</li>
  <li><strong>IME results.</strong> Insurers schedule IMEs with doctors who issue favorable reports supporting benefit termination.</li>
  <li><strong>Late notice.</strong> Insurance companies deny claims based on delayed reporting, even when notice was reasonable.</li>
  <li><strong>Coordination of benefits.</strong> Disputes arise when health insurance or workers' compensation may cover the same treatment.</li>
  <li><strong>Surveillance.</strong> Insurance companies may conduct video surveillance to catch injured claimants in activities inconsistent with their reported limitations.</li>
</ul>
<p>Our attorneys have extensive experience overcoming these defenses. We work with treating physicians, gather supporting evidence, and challenge improper denials through negotiation or litigation.</p>

<h2><strong>Third-Party Claims for Pain and Suffering</strong></h2>
<p>In addition to PIP benefits, Michigan allows injured victims to sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering damages—but only if your injuries meet specific legal thresholds. To pursue a third-party claim, you must prove:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Serious impairment of body function.</strong> Your injuries have seriously impaired an important body function, the impairment is objectively manifested, and it affects your ability to lead a normal life.</li>
  <li><strong>Permanent serious disfigurement.</strong> You have permanent scarring or disfigurement that is serious in nature.</li>
</ul>
<p>This threshold is heavily contested. Insurance companies fight hard to keep cases below it. Our attorneys build comprehensive cases with medical documentation, expert testimony, and detailed evidence of how your injuries affect your daily activities, work, and relationships.</p>
<p>If you meet the threshold, you can pursue additional compensation including pain and suffering, excess medical expenses, full lost wages, permanent disability, and loss of consortium.</p>
