<h2>Common Causes of Jackson County Bicycle Accidents</h2>
<p>Most bicycle accidents in Jackson County stem from driver negligence. Despite Michigan's efforts to improve cycling safety, distracted and careless motorists continue to pose deadly risks to cyclists sharing the road.</p>

<p>Common causes include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Distracted driving</strong> from cell phone use, texting, or other distractions</li>
  <li><strong>Failure to yield</strong> to cyclists at intersections or in bike lanes</li>
  <li><strong>Unsafe turns</strong> without checking for cyclists</li>
  <li><strong>Dooring incidents</strong> when drivers open car doors into a cyclist's path</li>
  <li><strong>Following too closely</strong> or passing without adequate clearance</li>
  <li><strong>Drunk or impaired driving</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Failure to obey traffic signals</strong> and stop signs</li>
</ul>

<p>While cyclists must follow traffic laws, the overwhelming majority of bicycle-car collisions result from motorist negligence. When a driver's carelessness injures you, Michigan law provides multiple avenues for compensation.</p>

<h2>Your Rights Under Michigan's No-Fault System</h2>
<p>Michigan's no-fault auto insurance system applies to bicycle accidents when a motor vehicle is involved. As an injured cyclist, you're entitled to Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits that cover:</p>
<ul>
  <li>All reasonable and necessary medical expenses</li>
  <li>Wage loss benefits (up to 85% of gross income)</li>
  <li>Replacement services for household tasks you can no longer perform</li>
  <li>Attendant care if your injuries require in-home assistance</li>
</ul>

<p>You file for these benefits with the auto insurance covering the vehicle that struck you. If that driver was uninsured, you file with your own auto policy. If you don't own a car, you file with the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan. Understanding your coverage before an accident occurs is critical—but our attorneys guide Jackson County cyclists through this complex system every day.</p>

<h2>Suing the At-Fault Driver for Full Compensation</h2>
<p>Beyond no-fault benefits, Michigan law allows you to pursue a third-party lawsuit against the negligent driver if your injuries meet the "serious impairment of body function" threshold. This requires proving an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life.</p>

<p>Bicycle accident injuries frequently meet this threshold given their severity. A successful third-party claim allows recovery of:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pain and suffering</strong> compensation</li>
  <li><strong>Excess economic damages</strong> beyond PIP limits</li>
  <li><strong>Reduced quality of life</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Permanent scarring and disfigurement</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Loss of enjoyment of activities</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>Our attorneys have extensive experience proving threshold injuries and maximizing third-party recoveries for Jackson County cyclists. We fight to hold negligent drivers accountable for the full scope of harm they've caused.</p>

<h2>Dangerous Roads and Intersections in Jackson County</h2>
<p>Certain Jackson County roads and intersections pose heightened risks for cyclists. High-traffic corridors, roads lacking adequate shoulders, and intersections with poor visibility increase the likelihood of serious accidents. If infrastructure defects or poor road maintenance contributed to your crash, additional parties beyond the driver may share liability—including municipalities and road commissions.</p>

<p>We investigate every aspect of your accident to identify all sources of compensation and all parties who share responsibility for your injuries.</p>

<h2>What to Do After a Jackson County Bicycle Accident</h2>
<p>The steps you take immediately after a bicycle accident can significantly impact your ability to recover compensation:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Call 911</strong> to report the accident and request medical attention</li>
  <li><strong>Document the scene</strong> with photos of your bicycle, the vehicle, road conditions, and visible injuries</li>
  <li><strong>Exchange information</strong> with the driver and obtain insurance details</li>
  <li><strong>Identify witnesses</strong> and get their contact information</li>
  <li><strong>Seek immediate medical care</strong> even if injuries seem minor—some serious conditions don't manifest immediately</li>
  <li><strong>Report the accident</strong> to the appropriate insurance company within one year</li>
  <li><strong>Contact an experienced bicycle accident attorney</strong> before giving recorded statements to insurance adjusters</li>
</ul>

<p>Insurance companies routinely minimize bicycle accident claims or argue that cyclists share fault. Don't navigate this process alone.</p>
