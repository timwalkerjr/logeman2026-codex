<h2>Common Car Accident Injuries in Ann Arbor</h2>

<p>The roads around Ann Arbor—from Stadium Boulevard to I-94 and US-23—see thousands of crashes each year. The injuries vary widely, but the most serious often include:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Traumatic brain injuries and concussions.</strong> Even mild traumatic brain injury can cause memory loss, inability to concentrate, personality changes, and depression. Emergency room doctors often miss these injuries while focusing on more obvious trauma.</li>
  <li><strong>Spinal cord injuries.</strong> Damage to the spine can cause chronic pain, paralysis, herniated discs, and permanent disability requiring lifetime care.</li>
  <li><strong>Broken bones and fractures.</strong> From simple breaks to compound fractures requiring surgery, these injuries mean time away from work and months of rehabilitation.</li>
  <li><strong>Soft tissue injuries.</strong> Whiplash, torn ligaments, and muscle damage may not show up immediately but can cause lasting pain and require extensive physical therapy.</li>
  <li><strong>Internal injuries.</strong> Damage to organs, internal bleeding, and abdominal trauma can be life-threatening and require immediate emergency treatment.</li>
  <li><strong>Psychological trauma.</strong> Many accident victims develop PTSD, anxiety around driving, and emotional distress that affects their quality of life.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Understanding Michigan's No-Fault System for Injury Claims</h2>

<p>Michigan's no-fault auto insurance system is unique and often confusing. After an accident, you typically file a claim with your own insurance company for Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits—regardless of who caused the crash. These benefits can cover medical expenses, lost wages, and other costs.</p>

<p>However, the 2019-2020 no-fault reforms changed the landscape significantly. Depending on your coverage level, your PIP medical benefits may be capped at $50,000, $250,000, $500,000, or remain unlimited. Understanding your coverage before an accident occurs is critical. Many Ann Arbor residents opted for lower coverage limits to save on premiums, only to discover their medical expenses far exceed their policy caps.</p>

<h2>Meeting the Serious Impairment Threshold</h2>

<p>Michigan law allows you to pursue a third-party lawsuit against the negligent driver if your injuries meet the "serious impairment of body function" threshold. This requires showing an objectively manifested impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life.</p>

<p>Successfully meeting this threshold allows you to recover compensation for pain and suffering, excess medical expenses, and other damages not covered by no-fault benefits. Our attorneys have extensive experience proving threshold injuries. We document how your injuries have changed your daily activities—whether you can no longer play with your children, perform your job duties, or enjoy the hobbies that once defined your life.</p>
